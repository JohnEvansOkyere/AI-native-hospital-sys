PART B · MINE ONLY

A doctor never prescribes before examining. I do not pitch. I ask, I listen, I take notes. I stay in the questions until they have felt the pain. The demo is the prescription, I earn it only after the diagnosis.

  • Ask, then go quiet. Whoever asks the questions controls the room. Let silences sit.
  • Take notes visibly. It signals their answers matter, and it feeds the pilot pitch.
  • Do not demo early. Stay in Examination and Diagnosis until Q5 and Q6 land.

Examination, understand their world

1After a patient finishes a cleaning or procedure, how do you currently make sure they come back for their next recall, and who does that work?
Listen for: "front desk calls when they have time", "we hope they rebook", "we don't really". That gap is the whole conversation.
2Out of every 10 patients due for their 6-month recall, how many actually come back on their own?
Get the drop-off number in their own words. Dental revenue lives on recalls, this number is money.
out of 10 return

Diagnosis, make the pain real and costly

3When a patient doesn't rebook, when do you usually notice, and how many simply disappear without you ever knowing why?
Surfaces the silent leak. Most never realise how many quietly vanish.
4In a typical week, how many booked appointments turn into no-shows, and what happens to that chair time and the dentist's hour?
An empty chair is paid-for time earning nothing. Let them say the number out loud.
no-shows / week
Empty chair hour costs:
5After an extraction, root canal, or implant, how do you currently know whether a patient is healing well, before they call in pain, or worse, post a bad review?
One of the few clinics with a near-perfect reputation. One bad recovery story online undoes a lot of trust. Let that sit.
6A patient who stays with Sonrisa is worth years of recalls, plus their children and family. When one drifts away after a single visit, what does that cost the practice over time?
The implication question. This is where pain becomes a number they feel. Do not rush it.

Prescription, paint the cure, then earn the demo

7If there were a way to remind every patient about their recall, follow up after every procedure, and bring back the ones who drift, all on WhatsApp, branded as Sonrisa, with no extra work for your team, would it be worth testing free for 60 days?
The trial close. A "yes" is permission to demo and present the free pilot. Then, and only then: "Let me show you exactly how it would look for Sonrisa."
